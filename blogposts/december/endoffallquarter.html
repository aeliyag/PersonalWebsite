End of Fall Quarter

A fall quarter recap covering coursework, projects, professional programs, and what I am focusing on next. I am also shifting these updates from weekly posts into longer newsletters.

Coursework

Religion

This course has changed how I think about religion and the role it plays in structuring societies. So far we have covered Durkheim and a set of origin stories that, across traditions, often include a flood: the Christian Bible, Babylonian myth, and Islam.

Islam also presented a geometric argument that I brought to my discrete mathematics professor. The idea used parallel lines converging to argue that the universe had a beginning and an end. I would push back that space and time are difficult to treat as lines that could actually sit parallel in that abstract sense, and that if they did, we would likely need another dimension to locate the points where they meet.

Religion and society keep folding into each other in ways I did not expect.

Discrete Mathematics

The course began as proof-heavy work: modular arithmetic, Bézout's identity, the division theorem, and related results. We have since moved into probability, computing probabilities across different setups. I have appreciated the instructor’s clarity and the structure of the material.

CS 144

We have been working through machine code, interfaces, memory, and data organization, and are currently focused on caches.

Music and Design

I originally expected this course to center on music theory and learning how to mix and use DAWs. Instead it has changed how I think about the experience of music itself. My professor’s background is experimental music, so I have been listening to pieces I would previously have dismissed as not music because they were difficult to sit with. The class has shifted that reaction. I recently attended my first experimental music concert and was struck by the orchestration and the full performance environment around it.

Having a portfolio site to include on applications has been useful. I built and launched it over the summer, added a seasonal homepage treatment, and connected Firebase for email collection.

HandsIn

HandsIn has been part of the Social Entrepreneurship and Innovation class under the mentorship of UChicago Booth professors Robert H. Gertner and John Edwardson. The team is applying to CNVC for funding to continue developing and growing the company. There has also been a pivot: from a healthcare-focused product to a general online ASL learning platform.

Tomeo

Two directions right now:

Textbook shopping. A scraper that would use search APIs and open-source scrapers to take a textbook list and find the cheapest options, in whatever format, for UChicago students.

Dorm shopping lists. Reverse-image list creation so first-years can build and find lists of items they want for their rooms.

The questions I keep returning to are founder-market fit and whether this is a real problem: what makes me the person to solve it? I do not have ecommerce experience, so I may need to do some list-building and learn affiliate marketing to get started. What I do have is being a university student who is willing to move quickly and approach the problem from outside the usual playbook.

On textbooks specifically: I have been doing customer interviews, and textbook purchasing has stood out as something many college students deal with. My hypothesis is that students would use a product that saves time, energy, and money when finding course books, but I will need an MVP to test that. Right now that looks like a spreadsheet-based search, with a simple app as the next step.

How I have been working

After returning from summer, this is the study structure that has been most effective:

Start the day with the hardest mental work. Go to classes, take walking breaks, and keep meals simple enough that they do not slow the rest of the day down. After classes I work on problem-solving: practice sets or essays. Starting can take a minute, but once I begin I can stay productive.

In winter it gets dark early, which makes it tempting to go straight back to the dorms. Instead I try to have a meeting or go to the gym so I stay engaged. After dinner and a short break, there is a good window for personal project work or more studying. Later in the evening I review for the next day: lecture notes in advance and readings, so the material is fresh for class.

I am also trying to keep social media to a minimum.
